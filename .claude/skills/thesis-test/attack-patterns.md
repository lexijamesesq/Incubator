# Attack Patterns — /thesis-test

The attack palette for break-condition authoring and the synthesis battery — drawn from operator case history and published stress-testing practice (Heuer/ACH, Klein premortem, Martin, McGrath, Rumelt, military COA); loaded at Step 1 co-draft, referenced by name in Step 3 lens charges and the Step 5 battery.

## §A — Lens-native attacks

An SME lens runs these when one is handed to it as a break condition. Each is a one-line question.

**Market / competitive**
- **No-procurement-category** — does any buyer vocabulary or RFP language name this category? No buyer words, no procurement home.
- **Incumbent-already-leads** — is the position open, or unspoken hygiene an incumbent already ships?
- **Vertical-incumbent displacement** — can entry dislodge a deep incumbent (density, satisfaction, switching cost)?
- **Free-incumbent switching-cost** — when the incumbent is $0-to-buyer, the cost argument is void; can the wedge win on workflow alone?
- **Competitive-velocity window** — time-bound the gap: how many months or acquisitions until the incumbent closes it?
- **Incumbent-countermove** — after the wedge ships, what is the incumbent's fastest cheap counter, and does the thesis survive the second move?
- **Integration-layer monetization** — does connective tissue command a premium, or get absorbed into platform expectations?
- **Market-not-sized** — if no analyst sizes the category, does commercial scale exist?

**Sizing / economics**
- **Sub-market ACV economics** — does the narrower buyer actually pay more, or is the sub-market bundled, smaller, and cheaper?
- **Willingness-to-pay observables** — separate stated demand from procurement demand: who has actually paid, at what observable price?

**Adoption / practice**
- **Protagonist workflow-reality** — walk a named human's real workflow; does it depend on this?
- **Adoption-motivation inversion** — the same statistic reads two ways: do they want it better, or want less of it?
- **Cultural-resistance** — does adoption fight identity or autonomy, not just features?
- **Logistics-table-stakes** — is the adjacent operational scope optional, or does its absence make this an add-on?
- **Content-integrity gaming** — does static, widely available content become a gaming surface?
- **Two-sided timeframe** — network effects take years; does the thesis horizon survive the chicken-and-egg?
- **Multi-vertical-at-launch** — breadth at launch reads shallow; the vertical playbook goes deep first.

## §B — Cross-cutting attacks

Orchestrator-run in the Step 5 battery; lenses individually pass what these catch.

- **Learning-outcome through-line** — permanent; phrasing from CLAUDE.md config key `incubator.learning_outcome_break_condition`.
- **Evidence-vs-interpretation** — show the seam between what a lens found and what you conclude.
- **Collapse-into-sibling** — a distinct bet, or a re-description of another portfolio item?
- **Altitude reframe** — is this a bigger thing at a different altitude? Re-size under the larger frame before ranking.
- **Time-bounded moat** — permanent barrier, or a closing window? Date it.
- **Historical reread** — reread relevant post-mortems as liabilities against this thesis.
- **Bad-strategy screen (Rumelt)** — does the thesis name a diagnosis and the obstacle, or only a destination?
- **Non-diagnosticity flag (ACH)** — would this HOLDS evidence read HOLDS for the rival theses too? Then it supports but does not discriminate; flag it before it decides a ranking.
- **Info-quality flag** — a BREAK or WEAKENS carried by a single uncorroborated source is fragile; flag it.

## §C — Selection rule

Every thesis gets the two universals — learning-outcome through-line + one market/competitive attack — plus one bespoke attack aimed at that thesis's weakest axis, chosen in the co-draft conversation, not applied as a uniform battery.

Break conditions are generated, not improvised: enumerate each thesis's must-hold conditions ("what would have to be true"), fragility-rank them, and derive break conditions from the most fragile. Then add the chosen palette attacks.
